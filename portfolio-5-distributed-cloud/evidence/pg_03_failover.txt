========== 1. Before: one primary, one standby ==========
  primary 5432: in recovery f, timeline 1
  replica 5433: in recovery t, timeline 1

========== 2. The primary is declared dead, so the replica is promoted ==========
  pg_promote() finished in 133 ms
  promoted 5433: in recovery f, timeline 2
  it now takes orders: order 600082 for customer 2

========== 3. Split brain: the old primary was never really dead ==========
  the old primary also takes an order: order 600050 for customer 1
  on 5432: order 600050 belongs to customer 1
  on 5433: order 600082 belongs to customer 2
  replicas streaming from 5432 now: 0
  Each side now holds an order the other has never seen, and nothing will reconcile them.
  The IDs differ by 32 only because a promoted server can skip ahead in its sequences (PostgreSQL
  logs sequence values 32 at a time). After that many more orders on 5432 the order numbers would collide.
  Real failover tooling stops (fences) the old primary first, then rebuilds it as a standby with pg_rewind.
